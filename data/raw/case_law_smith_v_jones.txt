MEMORANDUM OPINION AND ORDER

I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff Margaret Smith brings this putative class action against Defendants Apex Financial Services, Inc. ("Apex"), Robert Jones ("Jones"), and Meridian Bank, N.A. ("Meridian Bank"), alleging violations of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, 15 U.S.C. § 78a et seq., and the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, 15 U.S.C. § 80b-1 et seq. Before the Court is Defendants' Motion for Summary Judgment pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56. For the reasons set forth below, the motion is GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART.

II. BACKGROUND

A. Factual Background

Apex is a registered investment adviser that managed individual retirement accounts ("IRAs") for thousands of clients, including Plaintiff. Jones served as the Chief Investment Officer of Apex from 2018 through 2022. Meridian Bank served as the custodian for client assets managed by Apex.

Between January 2020 and December 2021 (the "Class Period"), Plaintiff and the proposed class members entrusted Apex with approximately $2.3 billion in retirement savings. Apex represented to its clients that it employed a "conservative, income-focused investment strategy" designed to "preserve capital while generating steady returns."

Contrary to these representations, Apex, under Jones's direction, invested substantial portions of client funds in highly speculative derivative instruments, including credit default swaps and leveraged exchange-traded funds. These investments were inconsistent with the stated conservative strategy and were unsuitable for clients seeking capital preservation.

When the market experienced significant volatility in early 2022, Apex's clients suffered aggregate losses exceeding $800 million. Many clients, including Plaintiff, lost 40% to 60% of their retirement savings.

B. Procedural History

Plaintiff filed the initial complaint on March 15, 2023. After briefing on a motion to dismiss, this Court dismissed certain state law claims but permitted the federal securities claims to proceed. See Smith v. Apex Financial Services, Inc., No. 23-CV-04521, 2023 WL 4521789 (S.D.N.Y. Sept. 12, 2023). Discovery concluded on February 28, 2025. Defendants filed the instant motion on April 1, 2025.

III. LEGAL STANDARD

Summary judgment is appropriate where "there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). A fact is "material" if it "might affect the outcome of the suit under the governing law." Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986). A dispute is "genuine" if "the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party." Id.

The Court must view all evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party and draw all reasonable inferences in that party's favor. Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986). The burden is on the movant to demonstrate the absence of any genuine issue of material fact. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 323 (1986).

IV. DISCUSSION

A. Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 Claims Against Apex and Jones

To establish a claim under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act and Rule 10b-5, a plaintiff must prove: (1) a material misrepresentation or omission; (2) scienter; (3) a connection with the purchase or sale of a security; (4) reliance; (5) economic loss; and (6) loss causation. Dura Pharm., Inc. v. Broudo, 544 U.S. 336, 341-42 (2005).

1. Material Misrepresentation

Defendants argue that Apex's marketing materials constituted mere "puffery" insufficient to support a securities fraud claim. The Court disagrees. Apex's representations that it employed a "conservative, income-focused investment strategy" designed to "preserve capital" were specific, quantifiable assertions about investment methodology—not vague expressions of optimism. See ECA & Local 134 IBEW Joint Pension Tr. of Chicago v. JP Morgan Chase Co., 553 F.3d 187, 206 (2d Cir. 2009).

The record contains substantial evidence that these representations were false when made. Internal communications produced in discovery reveal that Jones described Apex's actual strategy in internal emails as "swinging for the fences" and "going for maximum alpha regardless of risk." This directly contradicts the conservative strategy marketed to clients. A genuine issue of material fact therefore exists on the misrepresentation element.

2. Scienter

To establish scienter, Plaintiff must allege facts giving rise to a "strong inference" that Defendants acted with "intent to deceive, manipulate, or defraud." Tellabs, Inc. v. Makor Issues & Rights, Ltd., 551 U.S. 308, 319 (2007). The inference of fraudulent intent must be "cogent and at least as compelling as any opposing inference." Id. at 324.

Jones's internal emails, in which he explicitly acknowledged the mismatch between Apex's marketing materials and its actual investment approach, support a strong inference of scienter. The evidence suggests Jones was aware that clients believed they were receiving conservative management while he pursued an aggressive speculative strategy. Summary judgment on scienter is therefore DENIED.

3. Reliance

Plaintiff invokes the "fraud-on-the-market" presumption established in Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 (1988), which provides that investors rely on the integrity of market prices set by all available public information. However, this presumption applies to securities traded in efficient markets. Because IRAs managed by investment advisers are not traded in a public market, the fraud-on-the-market presumption is inapplicable here.

Nevertheless, Plaintiff has presented sufficient direct evidence of reliance. Plaintiff's deposition testimony establishes that she reviewed Apex's marketing materials before investing and relied specifically on the conservative investment strategy representations. This direct evidence of reliance defeats summary judgment on this element.

B. Investment Advisers Act Claims

The Investment Advisers Act of 1940 imposes a fiduciary duty on registered investment advisers to act in the best interests of their clients. SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, Inc., 375 U.S. 180, 194 (1963). This duty encompasses an obligation to provide full and fair disclosure of all material facts and conflicts of interest. Plaintiff's evidence that Apex pursued investment strategies inconsistent with client risk tolerances and without adequate disclosure raises genuine factual issues regarding breach of fiduciary duty. Summary judgment on this claim is DENIED.

C. Claims Against Meridian Bank

Plaintiff asserts claims against Meridian Bank for aiding and abetting the alleged fraud. To establish aiding and abetting liability under the securities laws, a plaintiff must show: (1) the existence of a primary violation; (2) knowledge of the violation by the alleged aider and abettor; and (3) substantial assistance in the primary violation. Woodward v. Metro Bank of Dallas, 522 F.2d 84, 94-95 (5th Cir. 1975).

Plaintiff's evidence regarding Meridian Bank's knowledge is insufficient to create a genuine issue of material fact. The mere fact that Meridian Bank processed transactions directed by Apex does not establish that Meridian Bank knew of or participated in any fraudulent scheme. Summary judgment in favor of Meridian Bank is therefore GRANTED.

V. CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants' Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED as to all claims against Meridian Bank and DENIED as to all federal securities and Investment Advisers Act claims against Apex Financial Services, Inc. and Robert Jones. The parties shall appear for a pre-trial conference on September 15, 2025 at 10:00 a.m.
